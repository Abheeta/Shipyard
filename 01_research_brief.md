# SHIPYARD — RESEARCH HANDOFF

## Project context
The user is in Week 2 of a Shipyard project. They need ~40 user conversations by the end of the week, a lightweight landing/waitlist page, a decision on whether the problem is worth solving, and then Week 3 building. The user wants to validate before committing.

## Current working hypothesis
People discover useful workout content on Instagram/TikTok/YouTube, save it, and later struggle to retrieve and actually use it.

Possible wedge:

> Share a workout video → AI turns it into a structured, searchable, usable workout.

Instagram should be treated as an initial input channel, not necessarily the enduring product boundary.

## Competing hypotheses
1. **Retrieval:** users cannot find a specific saved item later.
2. **Transformation:** users find it but must manually extract exercises, sets, reps, ingredients, steps, etc.
3. **Decision:** users have many saved items but do not know what to do today.
4. **Broader problem:** personal productivity, geeky/analytical, or B2B information-chaos problems may be more painful.

## Recommended current direction
Do not build a generic Instagram save/search product yet.

Do not build a full fitness social app yet.

Test the narrow bridge:

> **“I found this workout. Make it usable.”**

Input: share from Instagram/TikTok/YouTube.

Output: structured workout.

Potential later layers:

**library → planning → execution → tracking**

## Interview strategy
Suggested split:
- ~15 fitness users
- ~15 heavy saved-content / recipe users
- ~10 exploratory productivity/geeky/B2B users

Ask about recent real incidents. Do not ask only whether they would use a hypothetical product. Try to falsify the hypothesis.

## Critical questions

> “What’s the last workout video you saved that you actually used?”

Reconstruct:
- where it came from
- why it was saved
- when it was used
- how it was retrieved
- what happened before execution

Also ask:

> “If Instagram gave you perfect search across all your saved content tomorrow, would that completely solve the problem?”

This distinguishes retrieval from transformation/action.

## Founder/product-fit observation
The user seems especially interested in products where messy information becomes operationally useful:
- personal information → action
- geeky/analytical tools
- narrow B2B workflows

Potential founder sweet spot: making complicated information systems feel simple.

## Two-hour plan discussed
- 20 min: write hypotheses
- 20 min: make simple landing page
- 30 min: prepare interview questions
- 50 min: recruit/interview

Do not spend limited time polishing branding.

## Landing-page test

### Headline
**Turn workout videos into workouts you can actually follow.**

### Subtext
Found a great workout on Instagram, TikTok or YouTube? Share it with us. We’ll turn the video into a structured workout you can save, search and use at the gym.

### CTA
**Join the waitlist**

## Decision criterion
Strong signal = repeated painful behavior, ugly workarounds, meaningful frequency, or willingness to try/pay.

Weak signal = people saying the concept sounds cool without recalling a recent painful incident.
